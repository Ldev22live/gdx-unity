**Choices made without asking** (in TD-100):
- **The copy is made from the M3.10 terrain copy,** so the refresh has the editable ground too, as A3 describes.
- **Linked instances that keep the original ids,** so the scene behaves exactly like objects placed from the palette (apply, revert, sync).
- **The original's import scale becomes the instance's scale override,** to keep each tree's relative size.
- **A pine and a bush added to the starter pack** rather than leaving 42 generated objects in the refreshed scene.
- **The cabin stays generated.** A house slice belongs with R7a.

## 4. Known limitations and next steps

**Limitations:**
- **The look changes, not only the source.** Kenney's leaves are teal. Its tall rock is a spire, not the generated wide grey boulder. The bushes are leafy low-poly plants. Compare the before and after frames.
- **Only the valley is refreshed.** The desert, alpine, island and autumn copies keep their generated objects, and every landscape keeps its generated cabin or huts (a house slice needs R7a for its mesh).
- **The refreshed objects now block.** A Hero who used to walk through the generated trees goes around them. This is intended (TD-096), but it changes how the sample plays.
- **Web:** the scene draws, but the Hero does not walk (no genre profiles on the web, as for every terrain copy). M2b.7 records the per-asset web status.
- **Memory:** Java heap only.

**Next on the chart:** M2b.7 (the desktop and web manifest matrix). After that, R3c's remaining scenarios.
